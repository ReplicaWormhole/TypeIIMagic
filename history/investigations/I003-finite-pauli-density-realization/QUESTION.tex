\begin{question}
Can the finite-volume input used in the stabilizer-region manuscript be made
fully explicit in Lean: signed Pauli strings, the finite code projector and
density matrix, positivity and normalization of the resulting functional, and
the partial-trace identity for nested normalized signed-Pauli expressions?
Which exact finite-group restriction statement turns that identity into an
equality of local densities, and which infinite-volume GNS obligations remain
after it is checked?
\end{question}

\begin{assumption}[Fidelity rule]
The formalization must retain the sign of a Hermitian Pauli stabilizer and
must not infer that a surviving partial-trace term belongs to a smaller local
stabilizer group merely from tracelessness.  That identification must be
implemented or occur as an explicit equality of finite term families.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Approved scope.}
This investigation constructs the finite qubit matrix realization, a faithful
finite signed-Pauli stabilizer interface, the projector/density/state and
finite code-corner trace calculation, and a signed partial-trace theorem.  It
also packages the exact survivor equality used by the manuscript.  It bridges
the resulting finite group average to the abstract support theorem of I002
through an arbitrary unital star-algebra representation.

The signed partial-trace theorem is deliberately expression-level: its finite
term families have no hidden group law, positivity, or trace-one property.
The accompanying conditional nested-group layer identifies them with actual
larger and smaller stabilizer densities when supplied a tensor-coordinate
decomposition, an injective local-group inclusion, and the exact survivor
iff-condition.  Deriving that displayed contract from the countable-lattice
definition of local stabilizer groups remains future work.

\paragraph{Deliberate boundary.}
This investigation does not claim to construct the global countable lattice,
the quasi-local $C^*$-completion, a compatible GNS representation, the
strong-density extension of corner traciality, or the regional/complementary
product orbit.  Those obligations remain visible in I002 rather than becoming
unstated fields of a theorem called ``stabilizer''.
