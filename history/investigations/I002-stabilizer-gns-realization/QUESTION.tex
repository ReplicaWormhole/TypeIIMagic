\begin{question}
For the consistent stabilizer system and regional von Neumann algebra in the
current Overleaf manuscript, can the hypotheses used by the checked abstract
support-corner theorem be derived from the actual GNS construction?  More
precisely, can one formally verify the chain
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \text{local support projections }p_n
    &\longrightarrow q \\
    &\longrightarrow \text{a tracial, projection-faithful, full corner }q\cM_\G q \\
    &\longrightarrow \text{finite central fibers}?
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:question_chain}
\end{equation}
\end{question}

\begin{assumption}[Fidelity rule]
Every theorem in this investigation must use either an implemented
construction or an explicitly named hypothesis corresponding to a precise
manuscript step.  In particular, neither GNS cyclicity, strong closure,
Kaplansky density, nor direct-integral type classification may be replaced by
a project-local axiom or silently folded into an opaque ``stabilizer''
structure.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Approved scope.}
The investigation formalizes the finite group-average construction of local
supports, their monotonicity under nested stabilizer subgroups, the canonical
strong limit of the resulting decreasing projections, and the
vector-functional/dense-orbit implications yielding faithfulness on
projections and fullness in Lean.  It connects these results to the checked
full-tracial-corner theorem from I001, while keeping the GNS orbit and
corner-traciality hypotheses explicit rather than silently constructing them.
It includes a source-mapped technical note, compiled PDF, and fresh
independent mathematical and Lean/API reviews.

\paragraph{Known infrastructure boundary.}
Mathlib~v4.28 contains a GNS representation construction, but its source
records the cyclic-vector construction as unfinished.  It has no bundled
direct-integral or type-III-factor API.  The current formalization also does
not construct the tensor-product Pauli action, the compatible finite-volume
density matrices, or the local-to-von-Neumann corner-trace extension.  The
final record must distinguish the checked theorem from these exact upstream
blockers; it may not label the whole stabilizer theorem formally proved.
